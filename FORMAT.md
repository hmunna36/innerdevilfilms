# Format study: "LUST PART 2", Monk Mode Films

Reference: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=spITWqYE53Y — 3:31, 16:9, posted 18 Oct 2026.
The channel had 1.55k subscribers and the video had 63k views and 1.19k likes within a day.
That makes it a strong outlier: about 40 views for every subscriber.

Method: I watched it in the browser and measured every frame. I found 55 picture changes, about 45 real shots once the flashes are left out, and the brightness of every second. I also made contact sheets of a still every 2 seconds. The video has no caption track, so it has no narration: all the words are on screen. The sound was measured while it played (see "Sound"). Nothing was downloaded.

## 1. What the format is, in one line
A wordless animated short film, about 3½ minutes long, about one private struggle. The struggle is played by a shadow character. The story is told through a day counter, a calendar, a clock, phone screens, and short white lines of text on black.

## 2. The look
- **Palette.** Almost all of it is near-black: the average brightness is about 25 out of 255. The art is thin white and grey line drawing. There are only three accent colours, and each one has a job:
  - green ticks = clean days
  - pink or magenta = the urge (the shadow's outline, its eyes, the ring of light on the floor under it, the days it wins, and the counter after a reset)
  - red LED digits = the clock
- **Hero.** A cream, round-headed, simple figure in a grey hoodie with almost no face: two dot eyes and one line for the mouth. The acting comes from posture and from the eyelids.
- **Antagonist.** A pure-black silhouette of the same body, with a pink rim, a glowing pink grin, and a pink pool of light where it stands. It is small at first, crouched in a corner. It grows as the urge grows. In the end it is left behind in a doorway.
- **Sets.** About six, and they repeat:
  - the bedroom, seen side-on like a stage: the main set, about half the film
  - the city skyline at dusk or dawn: the only warm, bright place
  - a bathroom mirror
  - close-ups: phone, calendar, clock, shoes
- **Camera.** Mostly locked off. There are slow push-ins on the face and hard cuts to close-ups. Movement is low everywhere except the relapse and the comeback montage.

## 3. The words on screen (the whole "script")
Five kinds of text, each with its own place:
1. **Cold open.** White text on full black: one quiet line, then a bold second line that sets the hook. That takes 0 to 4 seconds, before any picture.
2. **Narrator line.** Small, white, centred near the bottom, 4 to 9 words, past tense, third person ("he").
3. **Turn line.** The same position in bold, on the beats that change the story.
4. **The shadow's voice.** Handwritten italic words floating next to the shadow. They build up word by word, two or three lines stacked, and the persuasive word turns pink. It speaks in second person ("you").
5. **Diegetic text.** Things inside the story world: a friend's chat thread, a notification, an email, the calendar month, the clock time.

There are roughly 25 lines in the whole film. The idea that carries everything is that the urge's voice slowly starts to sound like his own voice.

## 4. The counter system
- "DAY N" sits small in the top-left corner the whole time.
- It resets to a pink "DAY 0" at the fall, then climbs again (1, 3, 14, 47) through the comeback.
- The calendar close-up turns each count into something you can see: rows of green ticks, then a page of pink.
- One clock time comes back throughout (the hour of the fall). When that same time comes back near the end, it is the test.

## 5. Structure (3:31, matching the creator's own chapter list)
| Time | Beat | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:04 | Hook | Two lines on black: the long streak, and the danger he cannot see coming |
| 0:04–0:25 | Status quo | Day 300 routine: he wakes, runs, texts a friend, ticks the calendar. The shadow is tiny in a corner |
| 0:25–0:50 | Creep | The voice whispers again, now copying his own words. Small "just one" offers |
| 0:50–1:00 | False win | The counter climbs past 400 and good news arrives. "The counter said he was winning" |
| 1:00–1:23 | Bad day + justification | A bad day, then the voice's "you earned it" logic builds word by word |
| 1:23–1:52 | Fall | The clock time, a cut to black, the pink calendar day, DAY 0. The shadow is now huge. "Welcome back." One night turns into many |
| 1:52–2:22 | Shame + reach out | The mirror (the voice is him), a draft text to the friend, the voice saying "don't", then he sends the confession. The friend reframes it with the old number |
| 2:22–2:51 | Body remembers | He wakes before the alarm, laces his shoes, the brightest shot of the film, then runs with the friend. The counter climbs. Cuts every ~1.5 s |
| 2:51–3:09 | The test | The same clock time again, the same voice, now softer and wiser. He gets up and walks out through a lit door. The shadow stays in the dark |
| 3:09–3:20 | Thesis | Two lines on black: the urge is loud at the start and sounds like you at the end |
| 3:20–3:31 | Title card | The title in caps with a red underline, "PART 2", and a two-line tagline (white, then pink) |

Pacing: about 45 shots in 211 s, so a shot lasts about 4.7 s on average. Bedroom shots are held long; the fall and the comeback cut much faster.
Brightness journey: dark at first, a small warm lift on the false win, the darkest stretch at the fall, the brightest moment on the morning of the comeback, then dark again for the ending.

## 6. The packaging
- **Title:** `TOPIC PART 2 | An Animated Short Film`. One capitalised word for the struggle, then the same suffix every time.
- **Description:** a one-sentence logline, a one-line hook, a link to the previous part, chapter titles that are lines from the story (not labels), a "made from scratch" claim, a subscribe line, a helpline link, and 5 hashtags.
- It is a series ("Part 2"): the sequel to a video that worked, with the same hero, the same shadow and the same rules.

## 7. Sound
Measured live in the browser: level, brightness and bass share for each beat. The figures include the sound effects.
- **Never silent.** No stretch drops below −45 dB, from the first second to the title card. A bed of sound runs under every shot.
- **Dark and warm.** The spectral centre sits at 180–370 Hz the whole way, so it is low, pad- and piano-like. It is never bright.
- **The fall is the darkest and bassiest part** (1:23–1:52, bass share 0.86, centre 180 Hz). **The comeback is the loudest** (2:22–2:51, about −15 dB). The cold open and the ending are the quietest (about −23 and −20 dB).
- **Four hard hits** land on story beats:
  - 0:11, the first big sound, when the shadow is first noticed
  - 2:06, when the confession is sent
  - 2:23, when he wakes before the alarm
  - 3:21, when the title card lands
- Pattern: a soft bed under the story, the low end swelling as the urge wins, a lift for the comeback, and a single impact on the title.

## 8. What we copy and what we do not
**Copy (the format):** the length, the wordless story told in text lines, the day counter, the calendar and clock as story devices, the shadow as a personified struggle with one accent colour, the fall and comeback arc with a test at the end, the black thesis card, the title card, the title and description template, the series logic.
**Do not copy:** their hero design, their shadow's design, their lines, their stories, or their channel name. We need our own character, our own accent colour and our own words. Copying a 1.5k-subscriber channel's look one-to-one would also make us look like a clone to the same audience.

## 9. Note on our own channels
This is nearly the same territory as two things we already have: Almost Better Films (the white stickman on black, the day counter, the on-screen lines) and Quiet Wins (wordless films about one inner struggle, with an antagonist that personifies it). Which channel this belongs on is an open question.

## The format in one paragraph
A wordless animated film, 2:30 to 3:45 long, about one private struggle.
- **Look.** A near-black stage, thin white line art and white text on screen. There is ONE accent colour, amber, used on every film for the antagonist, the counter resetting and the key beats.
- **Characters.** Our own hero, and an antagonist that embodies the struggle. At first it seems separate from him; by the end it speaks in his own voice.
- **Counter.** It is on screen the whole time and counts what this particular struggle costs.
- **Words.** Three kinds, and each must move the story: a short narrator line in the third person ("he"), the antagonist talking to him ("you"), and text inside the story world (phone, calendar, clock).
- **Story.** Routine, then temptation that keeps growing, then the cost, then a test at an exact time we showed earlier, which proves whether he has changed.
- **Camera and sound.** Mostly still shots and close-ups that matter. Faster cuts at the turns. Sound that never goes silent but has a shape: low and heavy when he is losing, lifting when he recovers.
- **Ending.** A short black card with the closing thought, then our own title card.

## Owen (the hero, working name)
- **Body.** An all-white figure with thin dark outlines. The far arm and leg are a shade darker.
- **Face.** A circle head, dot eyes, short thin brows and a line mouth. He has eye bags only when he is worn out.
- **Rest of the build.** One body shape, tube limbs, mitten hands and round feet.

- **What makes him ours (none of it costs animation time):**

  - a bare neck
  - his own face
- **Do not move him any closer to the reference.** That rules out a bald head, a scarf or hood, and their face.

## The Pull (the antagonist, working name)
- **Body.** Owen's own rig drawn as a black silhouette, with an amber rim, lit eyes and a smile.
- **No legs.** Below the waist it narrows into one thread tied to whatever feeds it.
- **Glow.** One glow preset everywhere. Embers appear only at stage 4 and at the moment it wins.
- **Stages.** It grows in four: 1 a wisp, 2 a face, 3 leaning in, 4 it is him. It only gets his tuft at stage 4.
- **The thread has four states with fixed meanings:**
  - slack: he is resisting
  - neutral: it is waiting
  - taut: it is winning
  - released: he walked away

## The world
- **Greyscale and low contrast.** Only Owen, the phone and the Pull are bright.
- **Colour means something:** amber is the Pull, cool blue is the work he means to do.
