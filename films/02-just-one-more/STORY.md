# Film 02: JUST ONE MORE | The Phone Ate His Nights (LOCKED, 22 Oct 2026)

## Loglines

1. When his father works night shifts to pay for his last year of school, a student who can't stop watching
   "just one more" video must pass his final exam in seven days; the kind voice keeps telling him he has earned a
   break, until his phone dies and something else looks back at him from the black screen.
2. When he sleeps through a practice exam after a 4 AM scroll, a student has six days to read 600 pages, but every
   night the Angel says "just one more" until the Devil takes the phone, and the knock he ignores belongs to the
   person he is doing it all for.
3. When a student promises his father he will pass, he must break a habit that eats every night, but the only
   voice strong enough to stop him also stops him from opening the door.

**Picked: 1, with the knock from 2.** Sharpest stake (Dad's night shifts, one exam, one date) and the most
surprising cost (the hard voice keeps him at the desk while his father stands outside on his birthday).

## What changed from v1 (critic round 1: 5, 5, 4, 3, 6, 4, 5, 6)

- The stake now has its "because" and uses the title phrase: Dad: "Pass the exam. I can't do one more year of night
  shifts." Every "Just one more." after that is read against it. It pays off on the last call.
- The cost is something a call can't fix: Dad's birthday is the Thursday before the exam (a small cake drawn on the
  calendar in the first story shot). In Devil Mode Bean crosses it out without looking, and the knock is that
  Thursday. The note: "It's my birthday. I just wanted to see you. -Dad".
- The turn is his choice, not the battery's: when the phone dies, the Angel's last ember pushes the charger to his
  hand; the Devil says "Put it down."; he opens his fingers and lets the plug fall.
- The reveal is no longer a morph: three earlier moments replay as memory with the Angel and the Devil gone. His
  own thumb pressed Snooze, his own hands shut the drawer, and he said "One more page." himself while his father
  walked away. Nobody else was ever there.
- Lines fixed (no "pass on", no "I'll pass.", no "That's my boy", no Film 1 words); the knock is visible; the note
  is short and held 4.8 s; every text is held long enough to read; no black first frame; the overhead Devil moves
  flat across the picture, not toward the camera.

## Final critique (round 3, v3)

"v3 can ship. Every core element now works: the stake has a face; the turn is a struggle; the cost is a choice he
makes knowing it's Dad, and it can't be undone; the reveal happens on screen. What's left is execution: the
punchline of the reveal is the most rushed shot in the film; the Devil leaves off-screen before the shot that
depends on him being gone; the cat is the last placeholder." Best pair of lines: "Dad?" at the door rhyming with
"Dad? I passed." on the phone. (Its full notes and the fixes for them are summarised above.)

## What changed from v2 (critic round 2: 7, 7, 7, 6, 7, 6, 7, 8)

- The door is a choice he makes knowing it is his father: he whispers "Dad?", his chair scrapes back, he half-rises;
  the Devil's hand presses him back into the chair with "One more page."; he turns back to the book.
- The reveal swaps on screen: each memory opens in colour with the Angel or the Devil in it, then the colour drains
  and the figure dissolves, leaving his own thumb, his own hand, and himself whispering "Dad?" and then saying
  "One more page." in his own white. Before it, a reverse shot: the hallway light throws his long shadow across the
  dark room onto the empty chair where the Devil stood; the shadow has no horns.
- The phone is taken out of the drawer before it is plugged in (continuity).
- The turn keeps the plug in view: "Put it down." is a two-shot of the Devil's face and his hand with the plug; the
  release takes 1.8 s (the plug scrapes the port's edge, his fingers shake, open, the plug drops and bounces once).
  The Devil's birth is shorter (the smoke now runs under the face sliding in).
- The phone-on-his-face gag is replaced by a thematic one: his eyes close, the phone tips, and the autoplay ring's
  next video jolts him awake.
- Readability: every caption has a dark outline; the chat cuts to Dad's photo on the desk while he answers; the push
  in on the text box while he types and deletes "Sorry, Dad"; a 0.5 s shot of him turning the phone back over; the
  lock-screen notices held 2.2 s in short words; the opening calendar framed tight on Thursday and Friday; the
  rewind is a big central counter that flies to the corner; the calendar cross on Thursday happens late that night,
  after the knock (a cross marks a finished day); "School" everywhere (not "University"); "PRACTICE EXAM 9 AM";
  "So tonight is my last night shift."; the last "Just one more…" caption is cut (his thumb's reflex says it);
  the final image's red comes from the bedside clock's digits; the letterbox releases on the drawer slam.

## The new idea of this film

The phone's black screen is a mirror: the Devil is born in his own reflection when the battery dies. And the
title phrase passes between every voice: Dad ("one more year of night shifts"), the Angel ("Just one more."), Bean
("Just one more."), the Devil ("One more page."). The overhead shot of the bed is a bookend: the 3 AM nights in the
phone's glow, and at the end the same shot with the phone face down, charging, and him asleep.

## Characters, devices

Bean (the student), the Angel, the Devil. Dad is never seen: his photo on the desk (orange work vest, cap), his
chat avatar (the same vest), the shadow of his work boots under the door, his note. Counters (top left):
EXAM IN N DAYS, and PAGE 12 / 600 (his textbook), which does not move while the days fall and races in Devil Mode.
Captions are coloured by speaker everywhere, each with a dark outline: the Angel gold, the Devil red, Bean white,
Dad (from the phone) in a white speech bubble with a phone icon.

## The twist's clues (visible on a second watch)

1. The Devil is born in his own reflection (1.0, 73.7-76.9).
2. In the cold open the red "Put it down." flickers white, Bean's colour, for three frames.
3. The Angel's "Just one more." (28.4) comes out of his own mouth later (66.7).
4. The gesture rhyme: the Angel's wing pushes the phone to his hand (23.6), her ember pushes the plug (69.5), and his
   own hand drifts to the drawer the same way while his eyes stay on the book (92.1).
5. In the corridor (116.1) a horned shadow walks beside his own; in the lit room (136.7) only his own hornless shadow
   lies where the Devil stood.

Plants for the cost: the dog that waits at the door (29.8), the cake on Thursday (4.6, 43.8), Dad's photo (11.2), the
boots under the door (99.9), "Dad?" (101.4), his X through the cake (110.4), his feet over the note (114.3).
