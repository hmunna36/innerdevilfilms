# DOOMSCROLLING | It Eats Your Hours: the story (LOCKED 29 Oct 2026, after two critic rounds)

## Loglines
1. Every night the feed gives him something new, until the black glass shows him what it took.
2. Every night he gives the endless feed a few more hours, and every night the thing behind him eats them, months at a
   time: Grandpa's fishing trip, Christmas, the hospital call, until the phone dies and the glass shows him his own face,
   grown old. (CHOSEN: the cruelest cost, his grandfather's last good years, and the coldest line)
3. Every night it whispers "five more minutes," until five minutes is a year and the face in the glass is an old man's.

**Title:** DOOMSCROLLING | It Eats Your Hours. **Thumbnail hook:** IT EATS YOUR HOURS.
**Its device (new to this film):**
- **FEED**, an invented app: a blue tile with three white chevrons stacked downward (scroll down, forever). An endless
  column of posts made only of abstract pictures, heart counts and grey bars for text, scrolling up under his thumb, and
  a time chip at the top: "2h 14m tonight", "4h 51m", "7h 08m", "11h 26m" (red from 7h).
- **It eats his hours, visibly**: small glowing clock faces float out of his phone and drift into its open grin. A few on
  night 1, a stream on night 2, a torrent by night 4. When he breaks free they stream back into his chest.
- **Time goes by in months**: each night's card is a date (OCTOBER 14, DECEMBER 25, MARCH 3, JUNE 15) with the time small
  under it. His wall calendar loses its pages to it: on night 1 the pages tear off by themselves, THU 15, FRI 16, then
  SAT 17, the fishing Saturday, hangs a beat and is the first page its grin snaps through the window glass; November and
  December blur after it. On night 3 its long claw reaches in and spins the hands of his wall clock forward.
- **Grandpa has a face from the start**: a framed photo on his nightstand, a boy and his Grandpa (bucket hat, grey
  moustache) on a dock, holding up a fish. It sits in the phone's blue light; it is the first thing it rips into its mouth
  in the reveal; it falls back out when he breaks free; he stands it back up; the last act is that photo grown up.
- **His face pays too**: in each night's close-up he is a notch older (night 2 heavy bags, night 3 one grey streak at the
  temple, night 4 grey temples and a forehead line). In the black glass it finishes the job: his reflection grows old
  while he watches. When the hours stream back into him, so does his face.
- **The break**: he throws the dead phone into his nightstand drawer and slams it shut on its reaching claws.
- **The comeback**: push-ups, a run, the gym, then a book under the lamp instead of the feed; he stands Grandpa's photo
  back up; at 9:40 PM the shut drawer buzzes and slides open by itself, it attacks, and he deletes FEED; he texts Grandpa.
  WEEKS LATER they fish at dawn on the same dock as the photo (a new place). In the end, in the dark doorway, its claw
  holds out a glowing phone, still scrolling; for a blink the mirror shows his old face.

**Costs (the banners, each one alone on the phone for at least 1.6 s):** Grandpa ("Fishing Saturday? Like old times"),
Mom on Christmas night ("Merry Christmas. Grandpa waited."), Mom ("Grandpa's in the hospital. Call!"), then Grandpa's own
call, declined by his thumb, then Grandpa ("Haven't seen you in months, kid.").
**Length:** 1:58 (117.6 s), 16:9, 1920x1080, 76 shots, average about 1.55 s, none over 2.6 s but the end card (3.5 s)
and the title (3.8 s). The cut keeps its own rhythm: night 3, with two blows, runs a second longer than the others; the
reveal's ageing is one long push-in across a cut.
