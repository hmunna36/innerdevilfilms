# ENVY | It Showed Him Everyone Else (story v3, LOCKED 19 Nov 2026)

About 1:35. No narration: music, effects, handwritten words. The look of film 07 (the black stage, the cream people, the
black devil with the red glowing edge, `stage/rig.js` untouched).

**Structure: TWO OF HIM** (new to the channel; 07 was THE YEARS, 06 ONE NIGHT). The first night he gives his work to
it, the picture tears down the middle **through him**: one boy splits into two identical boys at two identical desks,
mirror images of each other across the tear. LEFT is the one who looks into its window; RIGHT is the one who turns the
window face down and keeps drawing. Their eyes never meet until the turn: the left boy's are on the window, the right
boy's on his page. Each half has its own page counter at the top: both say 29, then they pull apart (LEFT 0; RIGHT 120,
300). At the worst the tear slides over and the left half takes most of the frame. The turn: the two of him look at
each other for the first time. After it the right boy has given his pencil away: his half dims, his counter stays at
300, he holds his pile and watches, and only the left counter climbs; when it reaches 300 the tear stitches shut and
there is one boy again. The split is not on screen all the time: the night the window first opens, Max's show and Max's
front door are whole pictures.

**Place:** his desk under a lamp, a cork board above it; the floor where two boys draw together; Max's first show (a
gallery wall); Max's front door. **Light:** the right half lives in the warm lamp; the left half lives in the window's
green light, and its lamp goes out for good after the second round. **Accent colour: GREEN**, the window's (other
people's lives). Everything that is his own is warm: the lamp, his yellow pencil.

## In one line

At sixteen something shows him his best friend's best day in a little green window, and he gives it a page of his own.
From that night there are two of him: the one who keeps looking, who feeds it his pages until he has none, and the one
who turns the window face down and keeps drawing. When the window finally shows the left one a young man everyone loves,
holding a comic with his own monster on the cover, he looks across the tear and sees the 300 ordinary pages it never
showed him.

## The devil (the star)

- **It never says a word against him. It only admires everyone else.** Every line it says is praise for somebody who
  is not him: "look. isn't he amazing?" "and he's only fourteen." "look at all his new friends." It is a fan, an usher
  with a little lit window, a showman for other people's lives.
- **Its trick: the window.** A green-glowing frame it holds up for him. It shows only the prize: the crowd, the medal,
  the hearts floating up. It never shows the work.
- **Its rule, learnt without a word:** every page of his own that he gives up (crumpled, torn) it eats, and **its window
  grows**: a postcard, a book, a poster, a whole wall. And each time it **reaches into the window and puts on what it
  showed him**: Max's blue cap, the gold medal. It never gets bigger. It gets closer (behind his chair, then its arms
  round him, then its claws holding his head toward the light), and it dresses up as everyone else. Beaten, it is
  undressed: the medal falls off at the turn, the cap at Max's door.
- **Its manner:** sweet and adoring at first, then eager, then a grand showman presenting the window like a prize; caught,
  it pleads ("just one look."); at the end, starved, it is plain again, holding a window the size of a stamp, still
  praising someone else.
- **It eats his pencil.** In the second round it plucks the yellow pencil from behind his ear and eats it.

## The people

**Theo**, sixteen at the start (no age is shown on screen): a **yellow pencil behind his ear** (the left one loses it in round 2 and gets it back at
the turn). **Max**, his best friend: a **blue cap**. Theo draws monsters (a round black monster with a smile); Max draws
heroes. A few dim people at Max's show.

For the Shorts (9:16) the two halves are stacked top and bottom, like a reflection, instead of cropped.

## Why it is not THE BOTTLE

- The devil never grows and is never starved size by size: it is his size from the first night, it gets **closer** and
  **dresses up as everyone else**, and its **window** is what grows. Its words are praise, never lies about him.
- Two of him side by side, mirror-imaged across a torn line, two counters of pages (not one life in time jumps under an
  AGE counter). The comeback is the left boy catching up with the right one while the tear stitches shut, with a slip
  at 40 that he gets out of by comparing himself with his own page 1, not a giant shrinking day by day. No age on
  screen.
- Green against warm, split; a different cost (a friend's face at his own big day), a different turn (he sees his own
  monster on the prize, then the work behind it), a different tune (a sly tango), a different last line.

## Safety and truth

No phones and no apps: the window is the devil's own, green, with no logo. Comparing starts in the early teens for most
people; no age is shown. Nothing graphic. The right boy is tempted every day too (the leak never goes out) and gets rejected; the left
one slips at 40 and gets himself out. The comeback is a page a day for most of a year and a repaired friendship.
